| 1 | **Collusion‑Tie Formation** – *Farmer ↔ Sub‑station staff* | Both must *simultaneously* decide whether to enter a corrupt exchange. | 2 | **Capacitor / DSM Adoption Coordination** – *Farmer ↔ Farmer (same transformer)* | Adoption is only profitable if enough neighbours adopt in the same cycle. | 3 | **Authorization Choice (Formal vs. Informal Connection)** – *Farmer ↔ Staff* | Farmer decides to pay for an authorised line or stay informal; staff decides whether to grant a cheap informal deal or enforce the formal tariff. | 4 | **Staff Enforcement vs. Tolerance** – *Regulator → Staff* (sequential) | The regulator first sets the **monitoring intensity** (High / Low). Staff then decides to **enforce** or **tolerate** informal behaviour. Higher monitoring raises the expected sanction cost of tolerance. | **Game tree** (regulator moves first):<br>```\nRegulator\n ├─ High‑Monitor ──► Staff: Enforce (payoff 1,2) / Tolerate (payoff 3,4)\n └─ Low‑Monitor  ──► Staff: Enforce (payoff 2,3) / Tolerate (payoff 1,1)\n```<br>Numbers are ordinal (first = regulator, second = staff). High monitoring makes tolerance unattractive for staff; low monitoring makes tolerance attractive. | “Stochastic monitoring intensity” is listed as an exogenous driver (I.ii.c). “Enforcement run” occurs each month (Process overview §3). |
| 5 | **Groundwater Extraction Decision** – *Farmer ↔ Farmer (paired within transformer)* | Each farmer chooses **Full extraction** or **Restrain**. Payoffs depend on aquifer stress and any per‑unit tax. Mutual restraint is socially optimal; unilateral full extraction yields higher short‑term gain but accelerates depletion. | **Simultaneous matrix**<br>```\n                Partner\n            Restrain   Full\nFarmer  Restrain   (1,1)   (2,3)\n        Full       (3,2)   (4,4)\n```<br>(1) both restrain → sustainable yields (best); (2) farmer restrains while partner extracts → farmer gets moderate, partner high; (3) opposite; (4) both extract → low for both (depletion). | “Each connected farmer chooses between pumping at full rate and restraining extraction… the relative attractiveness of restraint rises as **aquifer stress** increases” (III.iv.a). |
| 6 | **Transformer‑Capacity Funding (Contribution vs. Free‑Ride)** – *Farmer ↔ Farmer* | Farmers must decide whether to **contribute** to the upgrade of transformer capacity (paying a private cost) or **free‑ride** on neighbours’ contributions. The “contribution” decision is part of the same sub‑model that links farmer‑staff capacity investment. |
| 7 | **Staff Investment in Capacity for a Tied Farmer** – *Staff → Farmer* (sequential) | Staff first decides **Invest** in additional transformer capacity for a specific tied farmer or **Not‑Invest**. If staff invests, the farmer can **Accept** the upgrade (receiving improved service) or **Reject** (e.g., because of repayment burden). | **Game tree**:<br>```\nStaff\n ├─ Invest ──► Farmer: Accept (payoff 1,1) / Reject (payoff 3,2)\n └─ Not‑Invest ─► Farmer: (payoff 2,3)   (status‑quo)\n```<br>Ranks: (1) best for both – capacity added, farmer benefits; (2) staff invests but farmer rejects – staff bears cost, farmer unchanged; (3) staff does not invest – staff saves effort, farmer remains with poor service. | “A staff member decides whether to **invest transformer capacity** on behalf of a tied farmer” (III.iv.a). The farmer’s willingness to accept is mentioned (“farmer’s willingness … independent of workload”). |
| 8 | **Institutional Push for DSM Adoption** – *Regulator ↔ Farmer* | The regulator may **apply a push** (subsidy / mandate) or **do nothing**. Farmers simultaneously decide to **Adopt** DSM (capacitor) or **Stay‑out**. Adoption is more attractive when the push is present; otherwise the coordination problem of §2 dominates. | **Simultaneous matrix**<br>```\n                Farmer\n            Adopt   Stay‑Out\nRegulator  Push   (1,1)   (3,2)\n           No‑Push (2,3)   (4,4)\n```<br>(1) push + adoption → highest payoff for both (policy meets goal, farmer benefits); (2) push but farmer stays out → regulator waste, farmer moderate; (3) no push, farmer adopts (rare) → farmer bears cost alone; (4) status‑quo. | “Any **state‑driven institutional push toward DSM adoption** is applied once per year at the last tick” (Process overview §4). This creates a distinct strategic interaction between the state and the farmer. |

\*When the ODD +D text specifies a *once‑per‑year* simultaneous choice (e.g., farmer‑farmer coordination, farmer‑staff collusion) we use a normal‑form matrix; when a decision is clearly *preceded* by another actor’s move (e.g., regulator sets monitoring, staff then enforces) we present a compact game tree.  

All eight situations are explicitly mentioned in the ODD +D description and each captures a unique strategic tension in the irrigation‑electricity governance system.
