**Action‑situations (AS) extracted from the ODD + D description**

|---|------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Capacitor‑joint investment (farmer‑farmer coordination)** | “Do I invest in a capacitor now and hope enough neighbours also invest, or do I wait and risk losing the collective voltage‑quality benefit?” | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**  <br>​Players: two representative farmers on the same transformer.<br>​Actions: **Invest (I)** / **Not‑invest (N)**.  <br>​Payoffs (Farmer A , Farmer B) : <br>​| I / I → (4 , 4)  <br>​I / N → (1 , 2)  <br>​N / I → (2 , 1)  <br>​N / N → (3 , 3) | The ODD text states that a farmer’s capacitor cost is only recouped *if enough farmers on the same transformer land on “invest” in the same cycle*.  This creates a pure coordination problem that is independent of any other actor – a classic IAD “action situation”. |
| 2 | **Transformer‑capacity contribution (free‑rider problem)** | “Should I pay to expand transformer capacity (bearing a private cost) knowing that non‑contributors will still enjoy the upgraded reliability?” | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**  <br>​Players: two farmers sharing a transformer.<br>​Actions: **Contribute (C)** / **Free‑ride (F)**.  <br>​Payoffs : <br>​C / C → (4 , 4)  <br>​C / F → (1 , 3)  <br>​F / C → (3 , 1)  <br>​F / F → (2 , 2) | The process overview mentions “transformer capacity/authorization stance” and that “contributors bear private costs while non‑contributors still enjoy reliability gains”.  The tension is the classic public‑good/free‑rider dilemma. |
| 3 | **Farmer‑staff collusion tie formation** | “Will the farmer offer a bribe and will the staff member accept, given their individual corruption level and the detection risk?” | **Sequential game (2‑stage tree)**  <br>​Stage 1 – Farmer: **Offer (O)** / **No‑offer (N)**. <br>​Stage 2 – Staff (if O): **Accept (A)** / **Reject (R)**; if N → outcome “Reject”. <br>​Payoffs : <br>​O‑A → (3 , 3)  <br>​O‑R → (1 , 2)  <br>​N‑R → (2 , 2) | The ODD specifies that a collusive tie forms only when *both sides* are independently willing; the farmer’s willingness depends on financial strain, the staff’s on corruption and workload, and the joint payoff is only realised when the offers match. |
| 4 | **Authorization choice (formal vs. informal connection)** | “Should I apply for a formal (authorised) connection, risking a fee and possible denial, or stay informal and rely on a collusive relationship?” | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**  <br>​Players: Farmer ↔ Staff (the staff member who can grant or deny). <br>​Farmer actions: **Formal (F)** / **Informal (I)**. <br>​Staff actions: **Grant (G)** / **Deny (D)**. <br>​Payoffs : <br>​F‑G → (4 , 4)  <br>​F‑D → (1 , 2)  <br>​I‑G → (3 , 3)  <br>​I‑D → (2 , 1) | The ODD describes a *choice between paid, formal connection or remaining informal*, with staff discretion that varies with collusion density.  The outcome depends jointly on the farmer’s request and the staff’s response – a distinct action situation. |
| 5 | **Staff capacity‑investment decision for a tied farmer** | “Do I allocate my limited workload to upgrade transformer capacity for a farmer I am tied to, or do I postpone the investment and risk losing the tie?” | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**  <br>​Players: Staff ↔ Farmer (tied). <br>​Staff actions: **Invest (I)** / **Not‑invest (N)**. <br>​Farmer actions: **Accept (A)** / **Decline (D)**. <br>​Payoffs : <br>​I‑A → (4 , 4)  <br>​I‑D → (1 , 3)  <br>​N‑A → (2 , 1)  <br>​N‑D → (3 , 3) | Sub‑model “staff decides whether to invest transformer capacity on behalf of a tied farmer” is explicitly described.  The farmer’s willingness to accept the upgrade and the staff’s workload‑dependent willingness create a bilateral strategic tension. |
| 6 | **Groundwater‑extraction decision (common‑pool dilemma)** | “Do I pump at full rate to maximise my short‑term yield, or do I restrain extraction to preserve the aquifer and avoid future cost spikes?” | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (representative neighboring farmers). <br>​Actions: **Extract (E)** / **Restrain (R)**. <br>​Payoffs (under moderate aquifer stress) : <br>​E‑E → (2 , 2)  <br>​E‑R → (4 , 1)  <br>​R‑E → (1 , 4)  <br>​R‑R → (3 , 3) | The ODD lists a sub‑model where “each connected farmer chooses between pumping at full rate and restraining extraction” and that “the relative attractiveness of restraint rises as aquifer stress increases”.  This is the classic common‑pool resource game. |
| 7 | **Social‑learning / imitation threshold** | “Given the number of neighbours who have already adopted capacitors, should I imitate them now (paying the cost) or wait for a clearer majority?” | **Sequential representation**  <br>​Nature first determines the *community state*: **Low‑adoption (L)** or **High‑adoption (H)** (threshold reached). <br>​Farmer then chooses **Imitate (I)** or **Stay‑out (S)**. <br>​Payoffs : <br>​L‑I → (1 , –) (cost without benefit)  <br>​L‑S → (3 , –) (no cost, status‑quo)  <br>​H‑I → (4 , –) (cost recovered, high benefit)  <br>​H‑S → (2 , –) (missed benefit). | The ODD states that “a transformer’s wider imitation pool only opens once its adoption count has jumped by a threshold within a single cycle” and that “farmers are drawn into an adoption pool … and may imitate at a fixed yearly probability”.  The farmer’s decision depends on an ex‑post community state – a separate action situation. |
| 8 | **Staff enforcement vs. tolerance** | “Do I allocate effort to enforce the formal connection rules (incurring monitoring cost) or tolerate informal connections, risking sanction if monitoring catches me?” | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**  <br>​Players: Staff ↔ *Monitoring agency* (exogenous but modelled as a strategic player for the purpose of the AS). <br>​Staff actions: **Enforce (E)** / **Tolerate (T)**. <br>​Agency actions: **High‑monitor (H)** / **Low‑monitor (L)**. <br>​Payoffs (ordinal) : <br>​E‑H → (4 , 4)  <br>​E‑L → (2 , 2)  <br>​T‑H → (1 , 3)  <br>​T‑L → (3 , 1) | The ODD mentions “enforcement run” each month and that “staff enforcement involves effort costs and potential sanctions if failures occur”.  The detection probability (high vs low monitoring) is an exogenous driver but is treated as a strategic contingency, yielding a distinct enforcement action situation. |
| 9 | **Farmer decision on paying an authorization fee vs. offering a bribe** | “Should I pay the official connection fee (guaranteed legal access) or try to obtain informal access by offering a bribe, risking detection and higher future costs?” | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**  <br>​Players: Farmer ↔ Staff. <br>​Farmer actions: **Pay‑Fee (F)** / **Bribe (B)**. <br>​Staff actions: **Accept‑Bribe (A)** / **Reject‑Bribe (R)** (if B).  <br>​Payoffs : <br>​F‑– (staff automatically grants) → (4 , 4)  <br>​B‑A → (3 , 3)  <br>​B‑R → (1 , 2)  <br>​(No‑bribe) → (2 , 2) | Although similar to AS 3, this situation isolates the *choice between a formal fee and an illegal bribe* as a distinct strategic decision that the farmer makes **before** any collusive tie is considered.  The ODD explicitly distinguishes “paid, formal connection” from “informal terms” and notes that “farmers with an existing tie to utility staff face better informal terms”.  Hence it is a separate action situation. |
