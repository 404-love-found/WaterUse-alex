### 1. Capacitor‑Adoption Coordination  
**Players:** Farmer A ↔ Farmer B (representative pair on the same transformer)  
**Strategies:** Invest (I) – pay adoption cost; Not Invest (N) – keep status‑quo  

|               | **B: I** | **B: N** |
|---------------|----------|----------|
| **A: I**      | (3, 3)   | (1, 2)   |
| **A: N**      | (2, 1)   | (2, 2)   |

*Interpretation:*  
- (3,3) – Both invest, threshold met → shared voltage‑stability benefit, high payoff for both.  
- (1,2) – A invests alone, pays cost but receives no benefit (rank 1); B free‑rides (rank 2).  
- (2,2) – No one invests; status‑quo (moderate payoff).  

**Justification:** ODD +D §III.iv.a – “a farmer who invests only realises the shared benefit **if enough farmers on the same transformer land on ‘invest’** within the same cycle; otherwise they pay the adoption cost with no return.”  

---

### 2. Farmer ↔ Staff Collusion Tie Formation  
**Players:** Farmer ↔ Sub‑station Staff (the staff member matched to the farmer)  
**Strategies:** Offer Collusion (C) / Refuse (R) for each side  

|               | **Staff: C** | **Staff: R** |
|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Farmer: C** | (3, 3)       | (1, 2)       |
| **Farmer: R** | (2, 1)       | (2, 2)       |

*Interpretation:*  
- Mutual offer (C,C) → collusive exchange realized, both obtain high payoff (e.g., informal credit, reduced fees).  
- Unreciprocated offer → proposer suffers (rank 1), responder gets modest benefit (rank 2) from avoiding risk.  
- Both refuse → status‑quo with moderate payoff.  

**Justification:** ODD +D §III.iv.a – “a collusive tie forms only when **both sides are independently willing**; willingness depends on corruption level, financial strain, and local detection risk.”  

---

### 3. Authorization Decision vs Regulatory Monitoring  
**Players (sequential):** Regulator → Farmer  

1. **Regulator** chooses Monitoring Intensity **High (H)** or **Low (L)** (affects detection probability).  
2. **Farmer** (observing the chosen intensity) decides **Authorize (A)** (pay formal connection fee) or **Stay Unauthorised (U)**.  

**Game tree (pay‑off ranks):**  

```
Regulator
 ├─ H ── Farmer
 │       ├─ A : (Reg 2 , Farmer 2)
 │       └─ U : (Reg 1 , Farmer 3)
 └─ L ── Farmer
         ├─ A : (Reg 3 , Farmer 1)
         └─ U : (Reg 2 , Farmer 2)
```

*Interpretation:*  
- High monitoring deters unauthorised use → farmer prefers A (rank 2) but regulator gets lower payoff (rank 1) because of enforcement cost.  
- Low monitoring makes unauthorised use attractive (farmer rank 3) while regulator tolerates lower compliance (rank 2).  

**Justification:** ODD +D §III.iv.a – “Farmers choose between **pursuing a paid, formal connection or remaining informal**; attractiveness of staying informal responds to **local collusion density** and **monitoring intensity** (risk of detection).”

### 4. Groundwater Extraction (Full vs Restraint)  
**Players:** Two neighbouring farmers sharing the same aquifer (same transformer).  
**Strategies:** Full extraction (F) – pump at maximum rate; Restrain (R) – limit pumping.  

|               | **B: F** | **B: R** |
|---------------|----------|----------|
| **A: F**      | (1, 1)   | (2, 3)   |
| **A: R**      | (3, 2)   | (2, 2)   |

*Interpretation:*  
- Both extract fully → severe draw‑down, low payoff for both (rank 1).  
- One restrains while the other extracts → restrainer gains higher long‑term benefit (rank 3) while extractor gets short‑term gain (rank 2).  
- Both restrain → moderate sustainable payoff (rank 2).  

**Justification:** ODD +D §III.iv.a – “Each connected farmer chooses between **pumping at full rate and restraining extraction**; the relative attractiveness of restraint rises as **aquifer stress** (energy cost) increases.”

### 5. Staff Capacity‑Investment Decision vs Workload  
**Players (sequential):** Staff → System (represented by “Capacity Availability” outcome)  

1. **Staff** decides **Invest (I)** in transformer capacity for tied farmers or **Not Invest (N)** (conserve workload).  
2. **Resulting system state** is either **Capacity ↑** (if I) or **Capacity ↔** (if N).  

**Pay‑off ranks (Staff, System):**  

- **I** → (2, 3)  (Staff incurs workload cost, system gains capacity).  
- **N** → (3, 1)  (Staff enjoys low workload, system suffers capacity shortage).  

**Justification:** ODD +D §III.iv.a – “A staff member decides whether to **invest transformer capacity** on behalf of a tied farmer; willingness declines with current workload.”

### 6. Informal‑Connection Choice (Tied vs Untied Farmers)  
**Players:** Farmer (with or without existing tie) ↔ Utility Staff (implicit, via “informal terms”).  
**Strategies (farmer):** Stay Informal (I) or **Seek Formal Connection (F)**.  
**Parameters:** Quality of informal terms depends on **collusion density** and **existing tie** (better for tied farmers).  

**Normal‑form (illustrative):**  

|                     | **Staff offers Good Terms** | **Staff offers Poor Terms** |
|---------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Farmer I (tied)** | (3, 3)                      | (2, 2)                      |
| **Farmer F**        | (1, 2)                      | (2, 1)                      |

*Interpretation:*  
- Tied farmer benefits from good informal terms (high payoff).  
- Untied farmer faces poor terms; formal connection becomes relatively more attractive.  

**Justification:** ODD +D §III.iv.a – “Farmers with an existing tie to utility staff **face better informal terms**; attractiveness of staying informal responds to **local collusion density**.”

### 7. Enforcement Effort vs Detection Risk (Regulator ↔ Staff)  
**Players:** Regulator (sets detection probability) ↔ Staff (chooses **Enforce (E)** or **Ignore (I)**).
