# Run 2 — openai/gpt-oss-120b

**Action‑Situation 1 – Farmer ↔ Staff Collusion Formation**  
**Strategic tension:** Both the farmer and the sub‑station staff must *simultaneously* decide whether to enter a mutually‑beneficial informal exchange (bribe/reciprocity) or to stay clean.  Success requires *concordant* offers.  

|                     | **Staff Accept** | **Staff Reject** |
|---------------------|------------------|------------------|
| **Farmer Offer**    | (2, 2) *Collude* – higher income for both, lower enforcement risk | (3, 1) *Farmer pays bribe, staff refuses* – farmer wastes money, staff keeps reputation |
| **Farmer Refuse**   | (1, 3) *Staff offers bribe, farmer declines* – staff loses potential gain, farmer avoids cost | (2, 2) *Both clean* – status‑quo payoffs (moderate) |

*Ordinal ranks (1 = best, 3 = worst) illustrate that the mutually‑cooperative outcome is jointly preferred, but a mismatch hurts the offering side.*  
**Justification:** Described in “collusion‑tie formation” (process overview) – a tie forms only when farmer’s offer and staff’s offer agree.

---

**Action‑Situation 2 – Capacitor/DSM Adoption Coordination (Farmer ↔ Farmer)**  
**Strategic tension:** Two neighbouring farmers on the same transformer decide *simultaneously* whether to invest in a capacitor (or other DSM) that yields a shared voltage‑quality benefit **only if enough neighbours adopt**.  

|                     | **Neighbour Invest** | **Neighbour Not‑Invest** |
|---------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| **Farmer Invest**   | (2, 2) *Joint benefit* – shared voltage improvement, cost shared via lower failure risk | (3, 3) *Solo cost* – pays adoption cost, receives no benefit |
| **Farmer Not‑Invest**| (3, 3) *Solo cost* – neighbour bears cost, farmer free‑rides on improved voltage | (1, 1) *Status‑quo* – no cost, no benefit |

*The matrix captures the classic coordination (stag‑hunt) game: “invest‑invest” is Pareto‑optimal, but unilateral investment is dominated.*  
**Justification:** From the “capacitor adoption pool” and the rule that a farmer’s investment pays off only when enough farmers on the same transformer also invest.

**Action‑Situation 3 – Formal Authorization vs. Informal Connection (Farmer ↔ Staff)**  
**Strategic tension:** A disconnected farmer chooses between paying for a *formal* (authorised) connection or remaining *informal*; the staff simultaneously decides whether to *enforce* the formal rule or to *tolerate* the informal link (often in exchange for informal benefits).  

|                     | **Staff Enforce** | **Staff Tolerate** |
|---------------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| **Farmer Formal**   | (2, 2) *Legal fee paid, staff gains compliance credit* | (1, 3) *Farmer pays fee unnecessarily, staff loses informal gain* |
| **Farmer Informal** | (3, 1) *Farmer faces penalty, staff gains enforcement reward* | (2, 2) *Both avoid costs, informal status quo* |

*Ranks show that when both prefer the same regime (formal‑enforce or informal‑tolerate) they obtain the higher joint payoff; a mismatch penalises the deviator.*  
**Justification:** Derived from “disconnected farmer chooses … formal connection or remaining informal” and “staff enforcement” steps.

**Action‑Situation 4 – Staff Investment in Transformer Capacity (Sequential: Staff → Farmer)**  
**Strategic tension:** The staff first decides whether to *invest* additional transformer capacity for a tied farmer; the farmer then decides whether to *accept* the formal regularisation (pay the fee) or to *reject* and stay informal.  

*Game tree (compact):*  

1. **Staff:** Invest ( I ) or Not‑Invest ( N )  
  a. **If I:** Farmer chooses **Accept (A)** → payoff (Staff 2, Farmer 2)
