| 1 | **Capacitor‑Adoption Coordination (Farmer ↔ Farmer)** | *Coordinate to invest in a shared voltage‑stability device* vs *Free‑ride on neighbours’ investment*. | 2 | **Farmer ↔ Staff Collusion Offer** | *Farmer decides whether to propose an informal exchange* vs *Staff decides whether to accept*. | 3 | **Connection‑Choice (Farmer ↔ Staff)** | *Farmer chooses between a formal authorised connection (pay fee, low risk) and an informal unauthorised connection (no fee, risk of penalty but possibly cheaper if collusion exists).* Staff’s willingness to facilitate the informal route affects the farmer’s payoff. | **Sequential game** (Farmer moves first, Staff responds)  <br>```\nFarmer:\n  1. | 4 | **Staff Capacity‑Investment (Staff ↔ Transformer‑Group)** | *Staff decides whether to invest effort/capital to increase transformer capacity for a tied farmer (or group of farmers).* Investment improves voltage reliability for all on the transformer but consumes staff workload. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (Staff vs “Collective Farmers” as a single opponent)  <br>```\n                 Farmers (Group)\n                 Accept   Decline\nStaff  Invest    (1,1)    (2,3)\n       No‑Invest (3,2)    (2,2)\n```<br>*(1,1) = capacity added, all benefit, staff incurs workload (acceptable). (2,3) = staff invests but farmers do not use the extra capacity (waste). | 5 | **Groundwater‑Extraction Decision (Farmer ↔ Farmer)** | *Each farmer decides between full extraction (high short‑term profit, higher aquifer stress) and restraint (lower profit, sustainability).* Pay‑offs depend on the neighbour’s choice because a collective tax may be triggered and aquifer draw‑down is shared. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**  <br>```\n               Neighbor\n               Restrain   Extract\nFarmer A  Restrain (1,1)   (2,3)\n          Extract  (3,2)   (2,2)\n```<br>*(1,1) = both restrain → low cost, sustainable aquifer (best). (2,3) = A restrains while B extracts → A bears cost of reduced water availability, B enjoys higher profit (worst for A). (2,2) = both extract → moderate payoff with higher long‑run stress. | Detailed in **III.iv.a** – “Each connected farmer chooses between pumping at full rate and restraining extraction… relative attractiveness of restraint rises as aquifer stress increases, and can be further discouraged by a per‑unit tax.” This is a classic **common‑pool resource** dilemma. |
| 6 | **Enforcement Decision (Staff ↔ Regulator)** | *Staff chooses to enforce formal rules (monitor, sanction) or to tolerate informal practices.* Enforcement incurs effort cost and possible reward (reputation) but raises detection risk for collusion; tolerance preserves informal gains but risks regulator sanction. | **Sequential game** (Regulator sets monitoring intensity → Staff decides)  <br>```\nRegulator (Nature): High‑Monitoring / Low‑Monitoring\nStaff (after observing intensity):\n   Enforce → payoff 1 (high monitoring) or 2 (low monitoring)\n   Tolerate → payoff 2 (high monitoring) or 1 (low monitoring)\n```<br>Ordinal ranking reflects staff’s preference for tolerance when monitoring is low and enforcement when monitoring is high. | Grounded in **II.ii.c** and **II.ii.h** – “Staff adapt enforcement effort to perceived oversight intensity… uncertainty enters through stochastic enforcement.” The tension is **formal compliance vs informal tolerance** under variable oversight. |
| 7 | **Institutional Push for DSM Adoption (Regulator → Farmers)** | *Regulator can introduce a state‑driven incentive (e.g., subsidy or mandate) for capacitor/DSM adoption at the end of the year.* Farmers decide whether to respond (adopt) given the incentive. | 8 | **Social‑Learning / Imitation Decision (Farmer ↔ Observed Neighbours)** | *Farmer decides whether to imitate a neighbour’s successful capacitor adoption (or not) after observing outcomes.* The payoff depends on whether the neighbour’s adoption was successful (i.e., enough adopters). | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (Farmer vs “Observed Peer”)  <br>```\n               Peer\n               Adopted‑Success   Adopted‑Fail   Not‑Adopted\nFarmer  Imitate      (1,1)            (3,2)          (2,2)\n        Do‑Not‑Imitate (2,2)            (2,2)          (2,2)\n```<br>*Interpretation*: Imitating a successful peer yields the best outcome; imitating a failed peer is worst; otherwise status‑quo. | Derived from **II.iv.a–c** and **III.iv.a** – “Farmers use a mix of heuristic and social‑learning rules: imitate successful peers…” This creates a **learning‑based coordination** tension distinct from the pure investment game. |
| 9 | **Transformer‑Burnout Risk (Collective Farmers ↔ Grid)** | *Each farmer’s load choice (high vs low draw) contributes to the probability of transformer failure.* If the transformer burns out, all suffer a large loss. The tension is between **individual short‑term gain** and **collective reliability**. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (representative pair)  <br>```\n               Neighbor\n               Low‑Load   High‑Load\nFarmer A  Low‑Load   (1,1)      (2,3)\n          High‑Load  (3,2)      (2,2)\n```<br>*(1,1) = both keep load low → low failure risk (best). (2,3) = A low, B high → A suffers higher risk, B enjoys higher output (worst for A). (2,2) = both high → moderate risk shared. | Implicit in **I.iii.a** step 3 (“transformer burnout checks and enforcement run”) and **III.iv.a** where “capacity/authorization stance” influences load. |10| **Authorization‑Capacity Allocation (Farmer ↔ Utility Staff)** | *When a farmer seeks a new connection, the staff decides how much transformer capacity to allocate (enough for reliable service vs minimal to save effort).* The farmer’s payoff depends on the reliability of the connection; staff’s payoff depends on effort. | **Sequential game**  <br>```\nFarmer: Request Connection\nStaff (after request):\n   Allocate Full Capacity → Farmer payoff 1, Staff payoff 2\n   Allocate Minimal Capacity → Farmer payoff 3, Staff payoff 1\n```<br>Ordinal ranks: 1 = farmer gets reliable service, 2 = staff bears higher workload, 3 = farmer gets poor service. | Stated in **III.iv.a** – “A staff member decides whether to invest transformer capacity on behalf of a tied farmer… willingness declines with their current workload.” This is a distinct allocation dilemma separate from the broader capacity‑investment AS (AS 4). |
