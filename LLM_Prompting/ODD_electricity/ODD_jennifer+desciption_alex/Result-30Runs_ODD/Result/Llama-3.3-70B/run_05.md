### Action Situation 1: Farmer Capacitor Adoption
- **Title**: Collective Action in Capacitor Adoption
- **Tension**: Farmers must decide whether to invest in capacitors, which improve electricity quality but require collective action to be effective.
- **Matrix/Sequential Representation**:
  - Normal Form Payoff Matrix:
    |  | Farmer 1 Invests | Farmer 1 Does Not Invest |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Farmer 2 Invests** | 3, 3 (Improved electricity quality for both) | 1, 4 (Farmer 2 benefits, Farmer 1 does not) |
    | **Farmer 2 Does Not Invest** | 4, 1 (Farmer 1 benefits, Farmer 2 does not) | 2, 2 (No improvement in electricity quality) |
- **Justification**: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers deciding to invest in capacitors. The payoff matrix shows that investing is beneficial only if both farmers invest, illustrating the collective action problem.

### Action Situation 3: Groundwater Extraction
- **Title**: Dilemma of Groundwater Overextraction
- **Tension**: Farmers must decide how much groundwater to extract, balancing their need for water against the risk of aquifer depletion and increased pumping costs.
- **Matrix/Sequential Representation**:
  - Sequential Representation (Game Tree):
    1. Farmer 1 decides to extract water at full rate or restrain extraction.
    2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's action and decides to extract water at full rate or restrain extraction.
    3. Payoffs are determined by the combined extraction levels, with overextraction leading to increased costs and potential losses for both farmers.
- **Justification**: This action situation represents the sequential decision-making process regarding groundwater extraction. The game tree illustrates how each farmer's decision depends on the observed actions of the other, reflecting the strategic tension in managing a shared resource.
