### Action Situation 1: Farmer Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Coordination for Capacitor Adoption
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to invest in capacitors, which improve electricity quality, but only if enough neighbors also invest.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Farmer 2 Invests | Farmer 2 Does Not Invest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 1 Invests** | 3, 3 (both benefit) | 1, 2 (Farmer 1 pays, Farmer 2 free-rides) |

#### Justification: This situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers who must coordinate their investments in capacitors to achieve improved electricity quality.

### Action Situation 3: Groundwater Extraction
#### Title: Extraction Restraint
#### Tension: Farmers must decide how much groundwater to extract, considering the trade-off between current benefits and future costs.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer 1 chooses extraction level (full rate or restraint).
2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's choice and decides on their extraction level.
3. Payoffs are determined by the combined extraction levels and aquifer stress.

#### Justification: This sequential situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers who must decide on their groundwater extraction levels, considering both the immediate benefits and the potential long-term costs of over-extraction.

### Action Situation 5: Transformer Capacity Investment
#### Title: Staff Investment in Transformer Capacity
#### Tension: Staff must decide whether to invest in transformer capacity for tied farmers, considering their workload and the potential benefits.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Staff decides whether to invest in transformer capacity.
2. Farmer decides whether to accept formal regularization if offered.
3. Payoffs are determined by the staff's investment decision and the farmer's response.

#### Justification: This sequential situation reflects the strategic tension between staff who must decide on investing in transformer capacity and farmers who must decide whether to accept formal regularization, considering the potential benefits and costs of these actions.
