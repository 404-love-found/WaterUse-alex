### Action Situation 1: Farmer Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Collective Action for Capacitor Adoption
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to invest in capacitors, which improve power quality, but only if enough neighbors also invest.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Farmer 1 Invests | Farmer 1 Does Not Invest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 2 Invests** | 3, 3 (Improved power quality) | 0, 2 (Farmer 2 benefits, Farmer 1 does not) |
| **Farmer 2 Does Not Invest** | 2, 0 (Farmer 1 benefits, Farmer 2 does not) | 1, 1 (No improvement in power quality) |

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers deciding to invest in capacitors. The payoff matrix shows that if both farmers invest, they both benefit (3, 3), but if only one invests, the other benefits more (2, 0 or 0, 2), and if neither invests, they both receive a lower payoff (1, 1).

### Action Situation 3: Groundwater Extraction
#### Title: Groundwater Extraction Dilemma
#### Tension: Farmers must decide how much groundwater to extract, balancing their own needs with the risk of aquifer depletion.
#### Sequential Representation:

1. Farmer 1 chooses extraction level (High or Low).
2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's choice and chooses extraction level (High or Low).
3. Payoffs are determined by the combined extraction levels and aquifer stress.

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers deciding how much groundwater to extract. The sequential representation shows that each farmer's decision depends on the other's choice, and the payoffs are determined by the combined extraction levels and aquifer stress.
