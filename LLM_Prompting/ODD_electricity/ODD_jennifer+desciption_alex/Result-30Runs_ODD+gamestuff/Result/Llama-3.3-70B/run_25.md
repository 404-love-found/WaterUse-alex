### Action Situation 1: Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Coordination for Voltage Stability
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to invest in capacitors, which improve voltage stability, but the benefits are strongest when adoption is coordinated among neighboring farmers.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:
|  | Farmer 1 Invests | Farmer 1 Does Not Invest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 2 Invests** | 3, 3 (Improved voltage stability) | 1, 2 (Farmer 2 benefits slightly, Farmer 1 does not) |
| **Farmer 2 Does Not Invest** | 2, 1 (Farmer 1 benefits slightly, Farmer 2 does not) | 0, 0 (No improvement in voltage stability) |
#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers who must decide whether to invest in capacitors. The payoff matrix shows that mutual investment leads to the best outcome, but unilateral investment may not be attractive due to the lack of noticeable improvement in voltage stability.

#### Title: Contribution to Transformer Capacity
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to contribute to authorized transformer capacity, which improves reliability for the local group, but costs are not always shared evenly.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:
|  | Farmer 1 Contributes | Farmer 1 Does Not Contribute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 2 Contributes** | 3, 3 (Improved reliability, shared costs) | 2, 4 (Farmer 2 benefits, Farmer 1 free-rides) |
| **Farmer 2 Does Not Contribute** | 4, 2 (Farmer 1 benefits, Farmer 2 free-rides) | 1, 1 (Transformer remains overloaded) |

The payoff matrix shows that mutual contribution leads to improved reliability and shared costs, but non-contribution can lead to free-riding and decreased reliability.

### Action Situation 3: Farmer-Staff Interaction
#### Title: Formal Compliance or Informal Exchange
#### Tension: Farmers and sub-station personnel interact under formal electricity rules and informal local relationships, where formal compliance involves authorization fees and staff effort, while informal exchange involves tolerance of unauthorized access or reciprocal favors.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer offers informal cooperation or seeks formal authorization.
2. Staff decides to enforce rules, tolerate informal access, or offer reciprocal favors.
#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers and sub-station personnel. The sequential representation shows that the farmer's initial decision affects the staff's response, and the outcome depends on the level of trust, oversight risk, and expected benefits.

### Action Situation 4: Groundwater Extraction
#### Title: Individual vs. Collective Extraction
#### Tension: Farmers must decide how much groundwater to extract, which affects not only their individual crop production but also the collective groundwater level and future pumping costs.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:
|  | Farmer 1 Extracts High | Farmer 1 Extracts Low |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 2 Extracts High** | 2, 2 (Short-term gain, long-term depletion) | 4, 1 (Farmer 1 conserves, Farmer 2 exploits) |
| **Farmer 2 Extracts Low** | 1, 4 (Farmer 1 exploits, Farmer 2 conserves) | 3, 3 (Sustainable extraction, shared benefits) |
#### Justification: This action situation highlights the tension between farmers who must decide how much groundwater to extract. The payoff matrix shows that mutual high extraction leads to short-term gains but long-term depletion, while mutual low extraction leads to sustainable use and shared benefits.

### Action Situation 5: Social Learning and Technology Adoption
#### Title: Imitation and Adoption of Capacitors
#### Tension: Farmers observe their neighbors' adoption of capacitors and decide whether to imitate, considering the visible benefits and potential risks.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer observes neighbor's capacitor adoption and outcome.
2. Farmer decides to imitate or not, based on perceived benefits and risks.
#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers who must decide whether to adopt new technology based on social learning. The sequential representation shows that the farmer's decision depends on the observed outcome of their neighbor's adoption, and the perceived benefits and risks of imitation.
