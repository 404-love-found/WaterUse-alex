**Action‑situations (IAD “strategic arenas”) extracted from the ODD + D description**

|---|-----------------|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Capacitor‑coordination (Farmer ↔ Farmer)** | Whether to invest in a voltage‑stabilising capacitor when the benefit materialises only if **enough neighbours on the same transformer** also invest. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (each farmer chooses **Invest (I)** or **Not Invest (N)**).<br><br>```\n               Neighbor\n            I            N\n      ---------------------------\n   I | (3,3)      (1,2)   \nF    |\n   N | (2,1)      (2,2)   \narmer\n```<br>*Explanation of payoffs:*<br>• (I,I): threshold reached → both enjoy high reliability (3).<br>• (I,N): lone investor bears cost, gets no reliability gain (1) while non‑investor free‑rides on the (still low) reliability (2).<br>• (N,N): no cost, but also no reliability improvement (2 each). | • “If enough farmers on the same transformer land on ‘invest’ within the same cycle, the shared benefit is realised; otherwise the investor pays the adoption cost with no return.” (ODD + D § III.iv.a).<br>• The description of “adoption pool” and “threshold” makes this a **pair‑wise coordination dilemma** that recurs for every transformer. |
| 2 | **Groundwater‑extraction coordination (Farmer ↔ Farmer)** | Two neighbouring farmers decide **Full extraction (F)** or **Restrain (R)**. The payoff to each depends on the other’s choice because over‑extraction raises the energy cost for everyone and depletes the aquifer. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (F vs R).<br><br>```\n                Neighbor\n            F            R\n      ---------------------------\n   F | (2,2)      (3,1)   \n   |\n   R | (1,3)      (3,3)   \n```<br>*Payoffs:*<br>• (F,F): Both get short‑term water but incur higher future pumping cost → intermediate (2).<br>• (F,R): Extractor gets high immediate benefit (3) while restrainer suffers loss (1).<br>• (R,R): Sustainable aquifer, low pumping cost for both → highest (3). | • “Farmers are paired within their transformer group each year; the relative attractiveness of restraint rises as aquifer stress … increases.” (ODD + D § III.iv.a).<br>• The “pairing” and “relative attractiveness” create a **public‑goods / tragedy‑of‑the‑commons** tension. |
| 3 | **Authorization request (Farmer ↔ Staff)** | Farmer chooses **Apply for formal (A)** or stay **Informal (I)**; Staff chooses **Grant authorization (G)** or **Deny (D)**. Formal access improves reliability but costs the farmer; staff incurs effort to record and maintain the connection. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (Farmer rows, Staff columns).<br><br>```\n                Staff\n            G            D\n      ---------------------------\n   A | (3,2)      (1,1)   \n   |\n   I | (2,3)      (2,2)   \n```<br>*Payoffs:*<br>• (A,G): Farmer gets reliable electricity (3) while staff bears modest effort (2).<br>• (A,D): Farmer pays fee but gets no connection (1); staff avoids effort (1).<br>• (I,G): Farmer stays informal but benefits from staff‑driven improvement (2); staff enjoys low enforcement cost (3).<br>• (I,D): Status‑quo (2,2). | • “Each disconnected farmer chooses between pursuing a paid, formal connection or remaining informal.” (ODD + D § III.iv.a).<br>• “Sub‑station personnel decide whether to invest transformer capacity … on behalf of a tied farmer …” (same section) – the grant/deny decision is the staff side of the same arena. |
| 4 | **Informal collusion (Farmer ↔ Staff)** | Farmer can **Offer informal cooperation (O)** or **Refuse (R)**; Staff can **Accept (A)** or **Reject (Rj)**. Mutual cooperation yields reciprocal benefits (easier electricity, small kick‑backs); unilateral offer leads to loss for the offering side. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**.<br><br>```\n                Staff\n            A            Rj\n      ---------------------------\n   O | (3,3)      (1,2)   \n   |\n   R | (2,1)      (2,2)   \n```<br>*Payoffs:*<br>• (O,A): Stable collusive tie – both obtain medium‑high payoff (3).<br>• (O,Rj): Farmer loses effort/possible penalty (1) while staff avoids risk (2).<br>• (R, A): Staff wastes effort trying to collude (1) while farmer stays neutral (2).<br>• (R,Rj): No collusion, baseline (2). | • “A collusion tie forms only where a farmer’s offer and their matched staff member’s offer agree.” (ODD + D § I.iii.a, III.iv.a).<br>• The text stresses *mutual willingness* and “local risk of detection”, making this a distinct bilateral exchange arena. |
| 5 | **Staff capacity investment vs farmer contribution (Staff ↔ Farmer)** | Staff decides **Invest capacity (I)** or **Not invest (N)** for a tied farmer; the farmer (after seeing the offer) decides **Contribute (C)** to the cost or **Free‑ride (F)**. The decision is **sequential** – staff moves first, farmer observes the investment offer. | **Sequential game** (first node: Staff I/N; second node (if I): Farmer C/F).<br><br>```\nStaff\n   ├─ I ──► Farmer\n   │          ├─ C : (3,3)\n   │          └─ F : (1,2)\n   └─ N ──► (2,1)\n```<br>*Payoffs:*<br>• Staff invests and farmer contributes → high reliability for both (3,3).<br>• Staff invests but farmer free‑rides → staff bears cost (2) while farmer enjoys benefit without paying (1).<br>• Staff does not invest → low reliability for both (2,1). | • “A staff member decides whether to invest transformer capacity on behalf of a tied farmer … In both cases a staff member’s willingness declines with their current workload; a farmer’s willingness to accept formal regularisation is independent of workload and comparatively low.” (III.iv.a).<br>• The *investment‑then‑contribute* timing is explicitly described, forming its own strategic arena. |
| 6 | **Staff enforcement vs farmer compliance (Staff ↔ Farmer)** | Staff chooses **High enforcement (H)** (incurs effort) or **Low enforcement (L)**; Farmer simultaneously decides **Comply (C)** (pay for authorized connection, respect rules) or **Defect (D)** (stay informal). | **Simultaneous 2‑player game**.<br><br>```\n                Farmer\n            C            D\n      ---------------------------\n   H | (3,2)      (1,1)   \n   |\n   L | (2,3)      (2,2)   \n```<br>*Payoffs:*<br>• (H,C): Reliable service, staff bears effort (3,2).<br>• (H,D): Farmer penalised (1); staff’s effort wasted (1).<br>• (L,C): Farmer enjoys low‑cost compliance, staff saves effort (2,3).<br>• (L,D): Status‑quo informal, moderate for both (2). | • “Staff enforcement involves effort costs and potential sanctions if failures occur, while inaction saves effort but increases reputational risk.” (II.i.b).<br>• The model schedules “enforcement run” each month (I.iii.a 3) and ties enforcement intensity to staff effort, making this a separate arena. |
| 7 | **Pump‑quality choice vs staff inspection (Farmer ↔ Staff)** | Farmer selects **Standard pump (S)** (higher upfront cost, lower failure) or **Low‑quality pump (LQ)** (cheaper, higher failure). Staff decides **Inspect (I)** or **Not inspect (NI)**. Inspection can catch low‑quality pumps and impose a penalty. | **Sequential game** (Farmer moves first, Staff observes pump type).<br><br>```\nFarmer\n   ├─ S ──► Staff\n   │          ├─ I : (3,2)\n   │          └─ NI: (3,3)\n   └─ LQ ──► Staff\n              ├─ I : (1,1)\n              └─ NI: (2,2)\n```<br>*Payoffs:*<br>• Standard pump → high reliability regardless of inspection (farmer 3). Staff prefers not to spend inspection effort (3) but gets a small benefit if inspection avoids future failures (2).<br>• Low‑quality pump → farmer gets low payoff if inspected (1) and moderate if not (2); staff gets low payoff if inspection catches a violation (1) and moderate if they avoid inspection cost (2). | • “Farmers choose between standard‑approved or low‑quality pump equipment … Staff decisions include inspection and enforcement …” (II.i.b, III.iv.a).<br>• The explicit *inspection* decision creates a bilateral game distinct from the broader enforcement arena because it hinges on the *type* of equipment chosen. |
| 8 | **Social‑learning imitation (Farmer ↔ Farmer)** | After observing a neighbour’s successful capacitor adoption, a farmer decides **Imitate (I)** or **Stay‑with‑status‑quo (S)**. The neighbour’s prior action is taken as given (the “observable”). Payoffs depend on whether enough imitators appear to reach the adoption threshold. | **Simultaneous 2‑player game** (both farmers either I or S).<br><br>```\n               Neighbor\n            I            S\n      ---------------------------\n   I | (3,3)      (2,1)   \n   |\n   S | (1,2)      (2,2)   \n```<br>*Payoffs:*<br>• Both imitate → threshold met → high payoff for both (3).<br>• One imitates alone → imitator bears cost without benefit (1), non‑imitator enjoys low cost (2).<br>• Both stay – no cost, no benefit (2). | • “A pool of farmers considered for adoption each year combines a small number drawn as prospective ‘experimenters’ … A transformer's wider imitation pool only opens once its adoption count has jumped by a threshold within a single cycle.” (III.iv.a).<br>• This captures the **local learning/visibility** mechanism separate from the pure coordination game (AS 1) because the decision is driven by *observed success* rather than direct coordination. |
