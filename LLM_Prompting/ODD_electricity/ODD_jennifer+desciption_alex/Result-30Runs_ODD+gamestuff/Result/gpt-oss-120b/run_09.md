### 1. Capacitor‑coordination Game (Farmer ↔ Farmer)

**Tension** – Farmers connected to the same transformer must decide whether to **Invest in a capacitor** (I) or **Not invest** (N). The payoff is high only when a critical mass on that transformer also invests (shared voltage‑stability benefit). A unilateral investment yields cost with little benefit.

**Matrix (ordinal ranks, 1 = best):**

|                | Neighbor I | Neighbor N |
|----------------|------------|------------|
| **Farmer I**   | (1, 1)     | (3, 2)     |
| **Farmer N**   | (2, 3)     | (2, 2)     |

*Explanation*:  
- (I,I): both get the coordinated benefit → best (rank 1).  
- (I,N): investor bears cost, gets little benefit → worst for investor (rank 3), neighbor enjoys unchanged service (rank 2).  
- (N,I): symmetric.  
- (N,N): status‑quo, moderate payoff (rank 2) for both.

**Justification** – “Capacitors can improve voltage stability… benefits are strongest when adoption is coordinated among farmers sharing the same transformer… If only one farmer installs a capacitor… unilateral investment unattractive” (see “Capacitor adoption and coordination”).

### 2. Authorization / Informal‑access Decision (Farmer → Staff)

**Tension** – A farmer chooses **Seek formal authorization** (A) or **Remain informal** (U). The staff then decides **Approve/Invest** (P) or **Tolerate informally** (T) / **Enforce** (E). The outcome depends on both moves.

**Sequential tree (Farmer moves first):**

1. **Farmer:** A → go to node 2; U → go to node 3.  
2. **Staff (after A):** P → farmer pays fee, gets reliable service (rank 1 for both).
