**1. Capacitor Coordination – Farmer ↔ Farmer**  
*Strategic tension*: A farmer’s payoff from installing a capacitor (cost incurred now, voltage‑stability benefit later) is high only if enough neighbours on the same transformer also install. Uncoordinated investment yields cost with little benefit; non‑investment yields a medium payoff if neighbours coordinate.  

**Normal‑form matrix** (Farmer A rows, Farmer B columns)  

|                | **Invest** | **Not Invest** |
|----------------|------------|----------------|
| **Invest**     | (3, 3) – coordinated benefit, shared reliability ↑ | (1, 2) – A pays cost, B enjoys marginal reliability |
| **Not Invest** | (2, 1) – B pays cost, A enjoys marginal reliability | (2, 2) – status‑quo, no extra cost, reliability unchanged |

*Ranks are ordinal (3 = best, 2 = intermediate, 1 = worst).*

*Justification*: ODD +D text (section “Capacitor adoption and coordination”) stresses that “benefits are strongest when adoption is coordinated among farmers sharing the same transformer” and that “unilateral investment is unattractive”. The game captures the coordination dilemma.

---

**2. Transformer‑capacity Contribution – Farmer ↔ Farmer**  
*Strategic tension*: Upgrading transformer capacity (or paying for an authorized connection) improves voltage for all users, but the contributor bears the private cost. Each farmer decides whether to contribute or to free‑ride on others’ contributions.

|                | **Contribute** | **Free‑Ride** |
|----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Contribute** | (3, 3) – reliable grid, shared cost | (1, 2) – contributor bears cost, free‑rider enjoys reliability |
| **Free‑Ride**  | (2, 1) – free‑rider saves cost, contributor bears cost | (2, 2) – no upgrade, unreliable grid for both |

*Justification*: The ODD description under “Transformer capacity and contribution imbalance” notes “When one farmer pays for authorization or capacity improvement, other connected farmers can still benefit … creates a free‑rider incentive”. The game isolates that public‑good tension.

---

**3. Formal Authorization – Farmer ↔ Sub‑station Staff**  
*Strategic tension*: A farmer may request a formal (authorized) connection (paying a fee) or stay informal. The staff can either **grant** the request (granting the fee, recording the connection) or **reject / enforce** (deny or penalise). The staff’s decision is influenced by oversight risk and workload; the farmer’s decision balances fee cost against penalty risk.

**Sequential representation**  

1. **Farmer moves**:  
   - **SeekAuth** (pay fee)  
   - **StayInformal** (no fee)  

2. **Staff observes Farmer’s move and chooses**:  
   - If **SeekAuth** → **Grant** or **Reject** (reject imposes penalty).  
   - If **StayInformal** → **Tolerate** or **EnforcePenalty**.  

**Resulting ordinal payoffs (Farmer, Staff)**  

| Farmer → | SeekAuth → Grant          | SeekAuth → Reject          | StayInformal → Tolerate   | StayInformal → Enforce |
|----------|--------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| **Farmer**| (3, 2) – legal access, modest staff effort | (1, 3) – fee lost, staff gains enforcement credit | (2, 2) – cheap electricity, staff saves effort | (1, 3) – penalty to farmer, staff gains compliance credit |

*Justification*: ODD +D “Authorization, enforcement, and maintenance” describes a “formal authorization decision” where “farmers choose… staff decides enforcement”. The sequential order reflects the real‑world timing (farmer applies, staff decides).

---

**4. Informal Collusion – Farmer ↔ Sub‑station Staff**  
*Strategic tension*: Both parties can engage in a reciprocal informal exchange (e.g., farmer offers a side‑payment, staff offers tolerance/extra load). Cooperation yields mutual benefit; unilateral cooperation leads to loss for the cooperating side.

|                | **Cooperate** (staff tolerates) | **Defect** (staff enforces) |
|----------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Cooperate** (farmer offers bribe) | (3, 3) – both gain (cheap electricity, staff side‑gain) | (1, 2) – farmer loses bribe, staff gets enforcement credit |
| **Defect** (farmer stays clean)    | (2, 1) – staff loses potential informal gain, farmer avoids cost | (2, 2) – status‑quo, no informal gain nor loss |

*Justification*: The ODD text under “Farmer and sub‑station personnel interaction” and “Informal collusion/tie formation” explains that “a collusive tie forms only when both sides are independently willing”. The matrix captures the classic coordination/reciprocity dilemma.

---

**5. Groundwater Commons – Farmer ↔ Farmer**  
*Strategic tension*: Each farmer decides **HighExtraction** (pump at full rate) or **Restrict** (pump less). High extraction raises short‑term yield but depletes the aquifer, harming future reliability for all. Mutual restraint sustains the resource; unilateral restraint while the other extracts yields a higher payoff for the extractor and a lower payoff for the restrainer.

|                | **HighExtract** | **Restrict** |
|----------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **HighExtract**| (1, 1) – rapid depletion, future costs ↑ | (3, 2) – extractor gains now, restrainer suffers |
| **Restrict**   | (2, 3) – restrainer saves water, extractor gains now | (2, 2) – sustainable use, moderate yields for both |

*Justification*: The ODD section “Groundwater extraction dynamics” notes that “individual high extraction dominates in the short run … mutual high extraction accelerates depletion”. The game isolates the common‑pool dilemma.

---

**6. Staff Capacity‑Investment Coordination – Staff ↔ Staff**  
*Strategic tension*: Two staff members assigned to the same transformer decide how much effort to invest in capacity upgrades ( **Invest** vs. **DoNothing**). Joint investment yields a reliable grid (high payoff for both) but each bears effort cost; if only one invests, the grid still improves but the investing staff bears disproportionate effort; if neither invests, reliability stays low.

|                | **Invest** | **DoNothing** |
|----------------|------------|---------------|
| **Invest**     | (3, 3) – shared reliability, balanced effort | (2, 1) – investor bears cost, colleague free‑rides |

**7. Staff Enforcement vs. Oversight Risk – Staff ↔ Nature (Detection)**  
*Strategic tension*: The staff chooses **EnforceStrictly** or **Tolerate**. Nature (probabilistic detection) determines whether the staff is caught for lax enforcement (penalty) or rewarded for strict compliance. This is modeled as a simple 2‑player game with Nature as the second player (chance node).

**Game tree**  

1. **Staff** chooses **Enforce** or **Tolerate**.  
2. **Nature** draws **Detected** (prob p) or **Undetected** (prob 1‑p).  

**Payoffs** (Staff, Farmer‑collective)  

- **Enforce / Detected** → (3, 2) – staff gains compliance credit, farmer suffers penalty risk.  
- **Enforce / Undetected** → (2, 2) – staff incurs effort cost, no extra benefit.  
- **Tolerate / Detected** → (1, 3) – staff penalised for laxity, farmers enjoy cheap electricity.  
- **Tolerate / Undetected** → (2, 3) – staff saves effort, farmers benefit.

*Justification*: The ODD mentions “staff decisions depend on oversight risk” and “enforcement runs each month”. Modeling the detection event as Nature preserves the 2‑player structure required by the task while staying faithful to the description.
