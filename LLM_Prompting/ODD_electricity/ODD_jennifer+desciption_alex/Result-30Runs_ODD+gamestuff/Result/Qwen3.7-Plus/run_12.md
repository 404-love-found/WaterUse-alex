# Run 12 — Qwen/Qwen3.7-Plus

### Action Situation 1: Coordinated Capacitor Adoption
**Tension**: Assurance Game / Coordination Dilemma. Unilateral investment in voltage-stabilizing equipment is unattractive because the shared reliability benefit only materializes if enough neighbors invest simultaneously. 
**Matrix**: 
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Invest | Not Invest |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Invest** | 4, 4 | 1, 3 |
| **Not Invest** | 3, 1 | 2, 2 |
**Justification**: The ODD+D text states that "some choices only pay off once enough neighbours act together" and that "if only one farmer installs a capacitor while neighbors do not, the local reliability improvement may be weak... making unilateral investment unattractive." The payoff structure reflects that mutual investment yields the highest reliability (4,4), while unilateral investment results in a net loss due to adoption costs without shared benefits (1,3 or 3,1). The best response to a neighbor's investment is to invest (4>3), but the best response to non-investment is non-investment (2>1), creating two equilibria.

### Action Situation 2: Transformer Capacity Contribution
**Tension**: Public Goods Dilemma / Free-Rider Problem. Capacity upgrades benefit all connected farmers, but costs fall unevenly. Unilateral contribution is privately unattractive because non-contributors free-ride on the improved reliability.
**Matrix**: 
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Contribute | Free-Ride |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Contribute** | 3, 3 | 1, 4 |
| **Free-Ride** | 4, 1 | 2, 2 |
**Justification**: The text explains that "some choices let one party's investment or restraint benefit everyone at that party's exclusive expense." When one farmer pays for authorization or capacity, "other connected farmers can still benefit... creating a free-rider incentive." The matrix shows that while mutual contribution improves shared infrastructure (3,3), unilateral contribution is exploited by the free-rider (1,4), making free-riding the strictly dominant strategy and leading to a suboptimal Nash equilibrium.

### Action Situation 3: Informal Exchange and Enforcement
**Tension**: Trust / Coordination (Assurance) Dilemma. Mutual informal exchange yields reciprocal benefits, but requires matched expectations. Mismatched expectations result in losses for the party offering cooperation.
**Matrix**: 
| Farmer \ Staff | Tolerate (Informal) | Enforce (Formal) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Offer Informal** | 4, 4 | 1, 2 |
| **Pay Formal** | 2, 1 | 3, 3 |
**Justification**: The text notes that "some outcomes depend on standing, reciprocal ties between farmers and officials" and that "informal exchange benefits both sides only when expectations are matched." If a farmer offers informal cooperation but staff enforce, the farmer loses (1,2). If staff tolerate but the farmer pays formal, staff lose potential informal rent (2,1). This creates two stable Nash equilibria: mutual informal exchange (4,4) and mutual formal compliance (3,3), reflecting the text's conclusion that "both formal compliance and informal exchange can persist as stable outcomes within the same social network."

### Action Situation 4: Groundwater Extraction
**Tension**: Tragedy of the Commons / Prisoner's Dilemma. Individual high extraction dominates in the short run by supporting crop production, but mutual high extraction accelerates aquifer depletion, raising future pumping costs and electricity demand.
**Matrix**: 
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Restrain | Extract Fully |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Restrain** | 3, 3 | 1, 4 |
| **Extract Fully** | 4, 1 | 2, 2 |
**Justification**: The text explicitly describes this archetype: "individual high extraction can dominate in the short run when others restrain, but mutual high extraction accelerates depletion and raises future pumping and electricity costs." The matrix captures this by making "Extract Fully" the dominant strategy (yielding 4 if the other restrains, 2 if both extract), leading to the socially suboptimal Nash equilibrium of mutual over-extraction (2,2) which degrades the shared groundwater basin.
