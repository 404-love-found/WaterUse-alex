# Run 11 — Qwen/Qwen3.7-Plus

**Action Situation 1: Capacitor Adoption Coordination**
**Tension:** Threshold coordination and public goods provision. A farmer only realizes the shared reliability benefits of investing in a capacitor if enough neighbors on the same transformer also invest in the same cycle. Unilateral investment incurs private costs without achieving the threshold for grid improvement.
**Matrix:**
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Invest | Not Invest |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Invest** | (3, 3) | (1, 2) |
| **Not Invest** | (2, 1) | (2, 2) |
**Justification:** Grounded in the ODD+D description of DSM adoption, where a commitment is confirmed "only where enough farmers on the same transformer land on 'invest' within the same cycle." If both invest, the threshold is met, yielding high reliability (3,3). If one invests alone, they bear the cost with no return (1), while the non-investor avoids the cost (2). If neither invests, both maintain the status quo (2,2).

**Action Situation 2: Formal Authorization vs. Informal Exchange**
**Tension:** Institutional compliance versus informal reciprocity. The farmer chooses between pursuing a paid, formal connection or remaining informal. The sub-station personnel choose between enforcing formal rules or tolerating informal exchange. Mutual informal exchange yields high private benefits but carries institutional risks.
**Matrix:**
| Farmer \ Staff | Enforce | Tolerate |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Formal** | (3, 3) | (3, 2) |
| **Informal** | (1, 2) | (4, 4) |
**Justification:** Reflects the farmer-staff interaction where "formal compliance can involve authorization fees... [and] staff effort," while "informal exchange can involve tolerance of unauthorized access." (Formal, Enforce) yields legitimate access and compliance (3,3). (Informal, Tolerate) yields the highest mutual private benefit via collusion (4,4). (Informal, Enforce) penalizes the farmer while costing staff effort (1,2).

**Action Situation 3: Transformer Capacity Contribution**
**Tension:** Free-rider problem in shared infrastructure. Upgrading transformer capacity improves reliability for all connected farmers, but the financial costs fall unevenly on those who choose to contribute. Non-contributors can free-ride on the reliability gains.
**Matrix:**
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Contribute | Free-ride |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Contribute** | (3, 3) | (1, 4) |
| **Free-ride** | (4, 1) | (2, 2) |
**Justification:** Directly maps to the text stating, "When one farmer pays for authorization or capacity improvement, other connected farmers can still benefit... creating a free-rider incentive." Mutual contribution shares costs for high reliability (3,3). Unilateral contribution leaves the contributor bearing disproportionate private costs (1) while the free-rider enjoys the benefits (4).

**Action Situation 4: Groundwater Extraction**
**Tension:** Tragedy of the commons. Individual high groundwater extraction is highly profitable in the short run for crop yield, but mutual high extraction accelerates aquifer depletion, which increases future pumping costs and worsens grid stress.
**Matrix:**
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Restrain | Extract |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Restrain** | (3, 3) | (1, 4) |
| **Extract** | (4, 1) | (2, 2) |
**Justification:** Based on the groundwater dynamics where "individual high extraction can dominate in the short run when others restrain, but mutual high extraction accelerates depletion and raises future pumping and electricity costs." Mutual restraint ensures sustainability (3,3). Mutual extraction depletes the aquifer, raising long-term costs for both (2,2).

**Action Situation 5: Staff Maintenance and Farmer Compliance**
**Tension:** Effort cost versus service reliability. Staff decide whether to invest effort in grid maintenance and enforcement or shirk to save effort. Farmers decide whether to comply with formal rules/pay fees or evade. Staff shirking saves effort but degrades the grid, penalizing compliant farmers.
**Matrix:**
| Staff \ Farmer | Comply | Evade |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Invest Effort** | (3, 3) | (1, 4) |
| **Shirk** | (4, 1) | (2, 2) |
**Justification:** Reflects the text noting "Staff enforcement involves effort costs... while inaction saves effort but increases reputational risk," and that "when staff withhold maintenance... farmers bear costs without receiving expected reliability." If staff invest and farmer complies, both achieve a stable outcome (3,3). If staff shirk but farmer complies, staff saves effort (4) but the farmer suffers poor service despite paying (1).

**Action Situation 6: Collusive Tie Formation**
**Tension:** Entry and matching in informal networks. A farmer decides whether to offer an informal favor/bribe to staff. Staff decide whether to accept or reject. Mutual exchange yields reciprocal benefits, but mismatched expectations (e.g., offering to a strict enforcer, or expecting an offer that doesn't come) result in losses.
**Matrix:**
| Farmer \ Staff | Accept | Reject |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Offer** | (4, 4) | (1, 2) |
| **Not Offer** | (2, 1) | (3, 3) |
**Justification:** Grounded in the rule that "a collusive tie forms only when both sides are independently willing." If both agree, they secure mutual informal benefits (4,4). If the farmer offers but staff reject (due to high oversight risk or low trust), the farmer loses the opportunity/bribe (1). If neither engages, they maintain a safe status quo (3,3).

**Action Situation 7: Pump Quality and Equipment Choice**
**Tension:** Private cost versus grid stability. Farmers choose between purchasing costly, standard-approved pump sets (which stabilize voltage and improve efficiency) or cheap, low-quality pumps (which save money privately but worsen local voltage quality and increase transformer burnout risk).
**Matrix:**
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Standard | Low-quality |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Standard** | (3, 3) | (1, 4) |
| **Low-quality** | (4, 1) | (2, 2) |
**Justification:** Derived from the entity attributes and bounded rationality sections, where farmers choose between "standard-approved vs. low-quality" pump sets. Low-quality pumps create negative externalities by worsening voltage stability. If both use standard pumps, the grid is stable (3,3). If one uses low-quality, they save money (4) while degrading the grid for the standard-pump user (1).

**Action Situation 8: Social Learning and Experimentation**
**Tension:** Information gathering versus free-riding on learning. Farmers decide whether to act as "experimenters" (paying adoption costs and risking failure/misattribution of outcomes) or "imitators" (waiting to observe neighbors' outcomes before adopting, thereby free-riding on the experimenter's information).
**Matrix:**
| Farmer A \ Farmer B | Experiment | Imitate |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Experiment** | (3, 3) | (2, 4) |
| **Imitate** | (4, 2) | (1, 1) |
**Justification:** Reflects the social learning mechanism where the adoption pool combines "prospective 'experimenters'" with those eligible to "imitate at a fixed yearly probability." If both experiment, they share the learning risk (3,3). If one experiments and the other imitates, the imitator free-rides on the information without the initial risk (4,2). If both imitate, no new information is generated, and diffusion stalls (1,1).
