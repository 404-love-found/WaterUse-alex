### Action Situation 1: Farmer-Farmer Coordination on Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Collective Action for Voltage Stability
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to invest in capacitors, which improve voltage stability, but only if enough neighbors also invest.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Farmer 2 Invests | Farmer 2 Does Not Invest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 1 Invests** | 3, 3 (Improved voltage stability) | 1, 2 (Farmer 1 bears cost, Farmer 2 free-rides) |
| **Farmer 1 Does Not Invest** | 2, 1 (Farmer 2 bears cost, Farmer 1 free-rides) | 0, 0 (No improvement in voltage stability) |

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers who must coordinate their investments in capacitors to improve voltage stability. The payoff matrix shows that mutual investment yields the best outcome, but unilateral investment can lead to free-riding and reduced incentives for individual farmers to contribute.

### Action Situation 2: Farmer-Staff Interaction on Informal Exchange
#### Title: Informal Access to Electricity
#### Tension: Farmers and staff must decide whether to engage in informal exchange, where farmers offer reciprocal benefits for tolerance of unauthorized access.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Staff Tolerates | Staff Enforces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer Offers Reciprocal Benefits** | 3, 3 (Mutual benefit from informal exchange) | 1, 2 (Farmer loses, staff gains from enforcement) |

#### Justification: This action situation captures the strategic tension between farmers and staff in informal exchange. The payoff matrix shows that mutual cooperation can yield benefits for both parties, but mismatched expectations can lead to losses for the farmer or staff.

### Action Situation 3: Sequential Decision-Making on Transformer Capacity Contribution
#### Title: Contribution to Transformer Capacity
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to contribute to transformer capacity, which improves reliability, but costs are not always shared evenly.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer 1 decides to contribute or not.
2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's decision and decides to contribute or not.
3. Payoffs:
	* If both contribute, they share the cost and benefit from improved reliability (2, 2).
	* If only one contributes, the contributor bears the cost, and the other free-rides (1, 3 or 3, 1).
	* If neither contributes, they both bear the cost of reduced reliability (0, 0).

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the sequential decision-making process of farmers contributing to transformer capacity. The representation shows how the first farmer's decision influences the second farmer's choice, leading to different payoffs based on the level of contribution and cooperation.
