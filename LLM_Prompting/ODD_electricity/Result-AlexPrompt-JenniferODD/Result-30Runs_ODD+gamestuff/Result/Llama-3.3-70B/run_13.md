### Action Situation 1: Farmer-Farmer Coordination on Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Collective Action for Voltage Stability
#### Tension: Coordination Dilemma
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Farmer 2 Adopts | Farmer 2 Does Not Adopt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 1 Adopts** | 3, 3 | 1, 2 |
| **Farmer 1 Does Not Adopt** | 2, 1 | 2, 2 |

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between two farmers deciding whether to adopt capacitors to improve voltage stability. The payoff matrix shows that mutual adoption (3, 3) is the best outcome for both, but if one farmer adopts and the other does not, the adopter bears the cost without full benefit (1, 2), illustrating a coordination dilemma.

### Action Situation 3: Sequential Decision on Groundwater Extraction
#### Title: Groundwater Overextraction
#### Tension: Sequential Exploitation
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer 1 chooses extraction level (High or Low).
2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's choice and then chooses extraction level (High or Low).
3. Payoffs are determined by the combined extraction level and its impact on groundwater depth and pumping costs.

#### Justification: This sequential action situation represents the dilemma of groundwater overextraction. The first farmer's decision influences the second farmer's choice, with both facing the temptation to extract more, potentially leading to overextraction and increased pumping costs for both.

### Action Situation 5: Staff Enforcement Decision
#### Title: Enforcement of Electricity Rules
#### Tension: Dilemma of Enforcement
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Staff decides on enforcement level (Strict or Lenient).
2. Farmers observe the enforcement level and decide on compliance (Comply or Not Comply).
3. Payoffs are determined by the enforcement level, compliance, and the resulting impact on electricity reliability and staff reputation.

#### Justification: This sequential action situation captures the staff's dilemma in deciding how strictly to enforce electricity rules. The enforcement level influences farmers' compliance decisions, with strict enforcement potentially improving reliability but also risking farmer backlash, and lenient enforcement potentially leading to overuse and system instability.
