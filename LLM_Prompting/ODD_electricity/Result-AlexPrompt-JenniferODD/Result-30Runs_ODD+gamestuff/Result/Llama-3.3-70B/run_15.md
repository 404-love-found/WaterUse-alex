### Action Situation 1: Farmer-Farmer Coordination on Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Collective Action for Voltage Stability
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to invest in capacitors, which improve voltage stability, but only if enough neighbors also invest.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Farmer 2 Invests | Farmer 2 Does Not Invest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 1 Invests** | 3, 3 (Improved voltage stability) | 1, 2 (Farmer 1 bears cost, no improvement) |
| **Farmer 1 Does Not Invest** | 2, 1 (No improvement, Farmer 2 bears cost) | 0, 0 (No improvement, both bear individual costs) |

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers who must coordinate their investments in capacitors to achieve improved voltage stability. The payoff matrix shows that mutual investment yields the best outcome, while unilateral investment is costly and ineffective.

### Action Situation 3: Sequential Decision-Making on Groundwater Extraction
#### Title: Groundwater Over-Extraction
#### Tension: Farmers must decide how much groundwater to extract, considering the potential for over-extraction and decreased water table levels.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer 1 extracts water (high or low amount).
2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's action and decides how much to extract.
3. Payoffs depend on the combined extraction levels and the resulting water table level.

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the sequential decision-making process on groundwater extraction. The representation shows that farmers observe each other's actions and adjust their own extraction levels, leading to potential over-extraction and decreased water table levels.
