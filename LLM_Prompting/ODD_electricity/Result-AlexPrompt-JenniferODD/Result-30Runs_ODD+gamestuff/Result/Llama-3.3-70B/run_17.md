### Action Situation 1: Farmer-Farmer Coordination on Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Collective Action for Voltage Stability
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to invest in capacitors, which improve voltage! stability, but only if enough neighbors also invest.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Farmer 2 Invests | Farmer 2 Does Not Invest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 1 Invests** | 3, 3 (Improved voltage stability) | 1, 2 (Farmer 1 bears cost alone) |
| **Farmer 1 Does Not Invest** | 2, 1 (Farmer 2 bears cost alone) | 0, 0 (No improvement in voltage stability) |

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the strategic tension between farmers who must coordinate their investments in capacitors to achieve improved voltage stability. The payoff matrix shows that if both farmers invest, they both benefit (3, 3), but if only one invests, the investor bears the cost alone (1, 2 or 2, 1), and if neither invests, there is no improvement (0, 0).

#### Title: Compliance and Informal Exchange
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to seek authorized access, while staff must decide whether to enforce rules or tolerate informal access.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Staff Enforces | Staff Tolerates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer Seeks Authorization** | 2, 2 (Formal access, staff effort) | 4, 1 (Farmer benefits, staff loses) |
| **Farmer Seeks Informal Access** | 1, 4 (Farmer loses, staff benefits) | 3, 3 (Informal exchange, mutual benefit) |

#### Justification: This action situation captures the strategic interaction between farmers and staff regarding authorization and enforcement. The payoff matrix shows that if the farmer seeks authorization and staff enforces, both get a moderate payoff (2, 2), but if the farmer seeks informal access and staff tolerates, they both benefit (3, 3), while mismatched actions lead to uneven payoffs.

### Action Situation 3: Transformer Capacity Contribution
#### Title: Contribution to Transformer Upgrades
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to contribute to transformer upgrades, which benefit all connected farmers, but the cost is borne by contributors.
#### Matrix/Sequential Representation:

|  | Farmer 2 Contributes | Farmer 2 Does Not Contribute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmer 1 Contributes** | 2, 2 (Shared benefit, shared cost) | 1, 3 (Farmer 1 bears cost, Farmer 2 free-rides) |
| **Farmer 1 Does Not Contribute** | 3, 1 (Farmer 2 bears cost, Farmer 1 free-rides) | 0, 0 (No upgrade, no benefit) |

#### Justification: This action situation reflects the dilemma of contributing to a public good (transformer upgrades). The payoff matrix illustrates that if both farmers contribute, they share the benefit and cost (2, 2), but if only one contributes, the contributor bears the cost while the other free-rides (1, 3 or 3, 1), and if neither contributes, there is no benefit (0, 0).

### Action Situation 4: Groundwater Extraction
#### Title: Groundwater Use and Conservation
#### Tension: Farmers must decide how much groundwater to extract, considering the trade-off between short-term benefits and long-term sustainability.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer 1 chooses extraction level (High or Low).
2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's choice and chooses extraction level (High or Low).
3. Payoffs are determined by the combined extraction levels and groundwater depth.

#### Justification: This action situation involves a sequential decision-making process, where each farmer's choice affects the groundwater level and the payoffs for both farmers. The representation shows that the decision is not simultaneous but rather sequential, with each farmer responding to the observed actions of the other.

### Action Situation 5: Social Learning and Capacitor Adoption
#### Title: Learning and Imitation
#### Tension: Farmers must decide whether to adopt capacitors based on observations of their neighbors' experiences and the perceived benefits.
#### Sequential Representation:
1. Farmer 1 adopts a capacitor (or not).
2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's outcome and decides whether to adopt a capacitor.
3. Payoffs depend on the adoption decisions and the observed outcomes.

#### Justification: This action situation highlights the process of social learning in the adoption of capacitors. The sequential representation captures how farmers learn from their neighbors' experiences and make decisions based on observed outcomes, reflecting the bounded rationality and learning constraints mentioned in the ODD+D text.
