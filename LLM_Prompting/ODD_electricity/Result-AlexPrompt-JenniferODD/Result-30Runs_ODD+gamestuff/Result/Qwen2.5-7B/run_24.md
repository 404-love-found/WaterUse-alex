### Title: Farmer Capacitor Adoption Decisions

### Tension: Farmer Capacitor Adoption Coordination

#### Matrix Representation:
|                | Farmer B Adopts | Farmer B Does Not Adopt |
|----------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Farmer A Adopts** | (2, 2)          | (1, 3)                 |
| **Farmer A Does Not Adopt** | (3, 1)        | (1, 1)                 |

#### Justification:
This action situation captures the strategic tension between two farmers sharing the same transformer. If both farmers adopt capacitors, they can achieve better voltage stability and pump efficiency, leading to a higher collective payoff. However, if only one farmer adopts a capacitor, the local reliability improvement might be weak or hard to attribute, making unilateral adoption less attractive. This reflects the bounded rationality and social learning mechanisms, where farmers rely on local experience and visible outcomes to make decisions.

### Title: Farmer-Staff Informal Exchange

### Tension: Farmer-Staff Informal Exchange Coordination

#### Sequential Representation (Game Tree):
```
Farmer
    |
    |--- Staff Tolerates (T) / Staff Enforces (E)
    |       |--- Farmer Tolerates (T) -> (2, 2)
    |       |--- Farmer Does Not Tolerate (N) -> (1, 3)
    |--- Staff Enforces (E)
              |--- Farmer Tolerates (T) -> (1, 1)
              |--- Farmer Does Not Tolerate (N) -> (3, 2)
```

#### Justification:
This sequential game captures the interaction between a farmer and sub-station personnel regarding informal access to electricity. If both parties are tolerant, they both benefit. However, if the farmer tolerates informal access and the staff enforces, the farmer faces penalties. If the staff tolerates but the farmer does not, the staff incurs reputational risk. This reflects the bounded rationality and social learning mechanisms, where both parties balance formal compliance and informal reciprocity based on trust networks and oversight risk.
