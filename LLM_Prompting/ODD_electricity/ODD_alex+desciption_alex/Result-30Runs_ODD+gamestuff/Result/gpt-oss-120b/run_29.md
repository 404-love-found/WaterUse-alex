**1. Capacitor‑coordination (Farmer A ↔ Farmer B)**  

- **Players:** Two adjacent farmers who share the same transformer.  
- **Strategies:** **Invest** in a capacitor (C) or **Not invest** (N).  
- **Payoffs (ordinal, 3 = best, 1 = worst):**  

|                | Farmer B: C | Farmer B: N |
|----------------|------------|------------|
| **Farmer A: C**| (3, 3)     | (1, 2)     |
| **Farmer A: N**| (2, 1)     | (2, 2)     |

*Explanation* – When both invest, voltage stabilises for all, giving the highest joint rank (3,3). A unilateral investment yields a private cost with little voltage gain (rank 1 for the investor, 2 for the non‑investor). When neither invests, the status‑quo is moderate (2,2). This matches the “assurance” game described in AS1.  

**Justification:** The ODD + D text states that “capacitors can improve voltage stability… benefits are strongest when adoption is coordinated among farmers sharing the same transformer. If only one farmer installs a capacitor while neighbors do not, the local reliability improvement may be weak… making unilateral investment unattractive.”

**2. Sequential Social‑Learning Adoption (Farmer A → Farmer B)**  

- **Stage 1 (Farmer A):** Choose **Invest (C)** or **Not Invest (N)**.  
- **Stage 2 (Farmer B) after observing A’s outcome:** Choose **Imitate** (adopt the same action) or **Stay** (keep previous behavior).  

|                | Farmer B: Imitate | Farmer B: Stay |
|----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **A = C** (successful) | (3, 3)            | (2, 2)         |
| **A = C** (unsuccessful) | (1, 1)            | (2, 2)         |
| **A = N**                | (2, 2)            | (2, 2)         |

*Explanation* – If A’s investment succeeds (visible improvement), B’s imitation yields the high joint rank (3,3). If A’s investment fails, B’s imitation is penalised (1,1). If A does not invest, B’s choice is irrelevant; the baseline (2,2) prevails.  

**Justification:** The description of AS2 says “a sequential social‑learning process in capacitor adoption in which each farmer observes a peer’s outcome and imitates only if that outcome ranks higher, so diffusion occurs only after a successful coordinated trial has been observed.”

**3. Transformer‑Capacity Authorization Dilemma (Farmer A ↔ Farmer B)**  

- **Players:** One farmer who can **Authorize/Upgrade** the transformer (A) and a neighbor who can **Free‑ride** (B).  
- **Strategies:** **Contribute** (C) or **Not Contribute** (NC).  

|                | B: C | B: NC |
|----------------|------|-------|
| **A: C**       | (3, 3) | (2, 4) |
| **A: NC**      | (4, 2) | (1, 1) |

*Explanation* – When both contribute, the transformer capacity rises, giving a high joint rank (3,3). If A contributes while B does not, A bears the private cost while B enjoys the upgraded reliability (A = 2, B = 4). If only B contributes, the reverse occurs (A = 4, B = 2). If neither contributes, the transformer stays overloaded (1,1).  

**Justification:** The ODD + D text describes “some farmers already contributed to authorized transformer capacity… other farmers seek access later or rely on informal connections. Capacity upgrades improve reliability for the local group, but costs are not always shared evenly… creates a free‑rider incentive for non‑contributors.”

**4. Farmer ↔ Sub‑station Staff Informal‑Exchange Coordination**  

- **Players:** Farmer (F) and sub‑station staff (S).  
- **Strategies:** **Offer Exchange** (E) or **Do Not Offer** (D).  

|                | S: E | S: D |
|----------------|------|------|
| **F: E**       | (3, 3) | (1, 2) |
| **F: D**       | (2, 1) | (2, 2) |

*Explanation* – Mutual exchange (E,E) yields the best outcome for both (3,3). If one offers while the other refuses, the offerer loses (1) and the refuser gets a modest baseline (2). If both refuse, the status‑quo (2,2) holds. This captures the “mutual‑exchange coordination game” (AS4).  

**Justification:** The ODD + D text notes “reciprocal benefit arises only when both engage in informal exchange; if either abstains while the other offers exchange, the offerer bears a loss while the abstainer reverts to baseline.”

**5. Formal‑Authorization vs Staff‑Investment Asymmetric Coordination**  

- **Players:** Farmer (F) decides **Formal Request (FR)** or **Informal Request (IR)**. Staff (S) decides **Invest (I)** in capacity/maintenance or **Withhold (W)**.  

|                | S: I | S: W |
|----------------|------|------|
| **F: FR**      | (3, 2) | (1, 4) |
| **F: IR**      | (4, 1) | (2, 2) |

*Explanation* – The best joint outcome (3,2) occurs when the farmer follows formal rules and staff invests, giving reliable service and modest staff effort. If the farmer makes a formal request but staff withholds, the farmer suffers (1) while staff saves effort (4). When the farmer asks informally and staff still invests, the farmer enjoys a high benefit (4) but staff bears the cost without fee (1). Mutual non‑investment (IR,W) yields the baseline (2,2).  

**Justification:** AS5 is described as “an authorization‑and‑investment asymmetric coordination game between a farmer (formal versus informal request) and staff (invest versus withhold capacity)… mutual formal cooperation is collectively optimal, but asymmetric incentives exist between legality and opportunism.”

**6. Enforcement Dilemma (Staff ↔ Farmer)**  

- **Players:** Staff (S) chooses **Enforce** (E) or **Relax** (R). Farmer (F) chooses **Seek Unauthorized Access** (U) or **Comply** (C).  

|                | F: U | F: C |
|----------------|------|------|
| **S: E**       | (2, 3) | (3, 2) |
| **S: R**       | (1, 1) | (4, 4) |

*Explanation* – If staff enforce while farmer seeks unauthorized access, staff gains (3) by upholding rules, farmer gets a low payoff (2). If staff enforce and farmer complies, both receive moderate payoffs (3,2). If staff relax and farmer seeks unauthorized access, both suffer (1,1) because the system degrades (risk of overload, penalties later). If staff relax and farmer complies, both enjoy the best outcome (4,4) – low enforcement cost and stable electricity.  

**Justification:** The text notes “staff decisions depend on oversight risk, trust networks… informal exchange benefits both only when expectations are matched… when staff enforce strictly while farmers attempt informal access, farmers face penalties; when staff withhold enforcement while farmers comply, both enjoy low costs.”

|----------------|------|------|
| **A: R**       | (3, 3) | (1, 4) |
| **A: O**       | (4, 1) | (2, 2) |

*Explanation* – Mutual restraint (R,R) yields the highest sustainable payoff (3,3). Unilateral over‑extraction gives the over‑extractor a short‑term gain (4) while the restrainer suffers (1). Mutual over‑extraction depletes the aquifer, reducing both (2,2). This matches AS6.  

**Justification:** The ODD + D description of AS6: “groundwater‑extraction prisoner’s dilemma… mutual restraint sustains yields but unilateral over‑extraction offers short‑term gain and accelerates depletion.”

- **Strategies:** **Maintain** (M) or **Shirk** (S).  

|                | S2: M | S2: S |
|----------------|-------|-------|
| **S1: M**      | (3, 3) | (2, 4) |
| **S1: S**      | (4, 2) | (1, 1) |

*Explanation* – Joint maintenance gives high reliability (3,3). If one maintains while the other shirks, the maintainer bears the effort cost (2) while the shirker enjoys the reliable service (4). If both shirk, the transformer fails (1,1).  

**Justification:** The ODD + D narrative states “staff decisions depend on effort costs, reputation, and the risk of transformer failure; maintenance effort is a shared public good that can be under‑provided when staff free‑ride.”

|                | B: S | B: L |
|----------------|------|------|
| **A: S**       | (3, 3) | (2, 1) |
| **A: L**       | (1, 2) | (2, 2) |

*Explanation* – When both buy standard pumps, voltage spikes are less likely and both enjoy higher reliability (3,3). If one buys a low‑quality pump while the other buys a standard one, the low‑quality user may cause voltage disturbances that hurt the neighbour (payoff 1 for the low‑quality user, 2 for the neighbour). Mutual low‑quality use yields a moderate baseline (2,2).  

**Justification:** The ODD + D text mentions “pump‑set type and quality affect voltage stability… low‑quality pumps increase overload risk, harming all users sharing the transformer.”
