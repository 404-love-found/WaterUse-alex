# Run 30 — Qwen/Qwen3.7-Plus

**Action Situation 1**
**Title:** Capacitor Adoption Assurance Game
**Tension:** Coordination problem with mutual cooperation Pareto-dominant but risky; unilateral investment yields no added private benefit, making individual adoption vulnerable to peer non-participation.
**Matrix:** 
Farmer A \ Farmer B | Invest | Not Invest
Invest               | 4, 4   | 1, 3
Not Invest           | 3, 1   | 2, 2
**Justification:** Represents AS1. Mutual investment yields shared improvement (4,4), but unilateral investment yields no private benefit (1,3), creating an assurance game where mutual cooperation is optimal but risky if the peer does not invest.

**Action Situation 2**
**Title:** Sequential Social Learning in Capacitor Adoption
**Tension:** Diffusion of technology depends on observing a successful coordinated trial; premature imitation of failed or unilateral adoption leads to suboptimal outcomes.
**Sequential Representation:** 
1. Farmer 1 chooses [Invest / Not Invest].
2. Farmer 2 observes outcome and chooses [Imitate / Not Imitate].
- If F1 Invests: F2 Imitates (3,3) | F2 Not Imitate (4,1)
- If F1 Not Invest: F2 Imitates (1,2) | F2 Not Imitate (2,2)
**Justification:** Represents AS2. Farmer 2 observes Farmer 1's outcome and imitates only if it ranks higher. Diffusion occurs only after a successful coordinated trial has been observed, preventing blind imitation of failed unilateral adoption.

**Action Situation 3**
**Title:** Asymmetric Transformer-Capacity Authorization Dilemma
**Tension:** Free-rider incentive where one farmer's authorization benefits both by raising voltage quality, but costs fall solely on the authorizer, creating uneven payoffs.
**Matrix:** 
Farmer A \ Farmer B | Authorize | Not Authorize
Authorize           | 3, 3      | 1, 4
Not Authorize       | 4, 1      | 2, 2
**Justification:** Represents AS3. If only one invests, the contributor bears the cost (1) while the non-investor free-rides and benefits more (4). If neither invests, both remain at a low but non-zero baseline (2).

**Action Situation 4**
**Title:** Mutual-Exchange Coordination Game
**Tension:** Reciprocal benefit arises only when both engage in informal exchange; a unilateral offer results in a loss for the offerer while the abstainer reverts to baseline.
**Matrix:** 
Farmer \ Staff | Exchange | Abstain
Exchange       | 3, 3     | 1, 2
Abstain        | 2, 1     | 2, 2
**Justification:** Represents AS4. Only matched cooperation yields mutual gain (3,3). If one abstains, the offerer bears a loss (1) while the abstainer reverts to baseline (2), making it a pure coordination game reliant on mutual trust.

**Action Situation 5**
**Title:** Authorization-and-Investment Asymmetric Coordination Game
**Tension:** Asymmetric incentives between legality and opportunism; formal cooperation is collectively optimal but staff bear the investment burden, while informal requests yield higher farmer gains but no formal fee for staff.
**Matrix:** 
Farmer \ Staff | Invest | Withhold
Formal Request | 3, 2   | 1, 3
Informal Req.  | 4, 1   | 2, 2
**Justification:** Represents AS5. Mutual formal cooperation is optimal (3,2), but an informal request with staff investment gives the farmer more (4) while staff bear the cost without the fee (1). A formal request with staff withholding causes farmer loss (1) and staff saves effort (3).

**Action Situation 6**
**Title:** Groundwater-Extraction Prisoner’s Dilemma
**Tension:** Common-pool resource extraction where mutual restraint sustains yields, but unilateral over-extraction offers short-term individual gain at the expense of long-term aquifer depletion.
**Matrix:** 
Farmer A \ Farmer B | Restrain | Over-extract
Restrain            | 3, 3     | 1, 4
Over-extract        | 4, 1     | 2, 2
**Justification:** Represents AS6. Mutual restraint sustains yields (3,3), but unilateral over-extraction offers short-term gain (4) and accelerates depletion, leaving the restraining farmer with a low payoff (1). Mutual over-extraction leads to degraded baseline yields (2).
