# Run 11 — Qwen/Qwen3.7-Plus

**Action Situation 1: Capacitor Adoption Assurance Game**
*   **Title**: Capacitor Adoption Assurance Game (Farmer-Farmer)
*   **Tension**: Assurance and coordination under risk, where mutual cooperation is Pareto-dominant but unilateral investment is risky due to lack of private benefit.
*   **Matrix**: 
    | Farmer 1 \ Farmer 2 | Invest | Not Invest |
    | :--- | :---: | :---: |
    | **Invest** | 3, 3 | 1, 2 |
    | **Not Invest** | 2, 1 | 2, 2 |
*   **Justification**: Grounded in AS1. Mutual investment in voltage-stabilizing equipment yields shared grid improvement (3,3). Unilateral investment yields no added private benefit for the investor, who merely bears the cost (1), while the non-investor enjoys the baseline (2). Mutual non-investment maintains the low baseline (2,2).

**Action Situation 2: Sequential Social-Learning in Capacitor Adoption**
*   **Title**: Sequential Social-Learning Process (Farmer-Farmer)
*   **Tension**: Information asymmetry and sequential technology diffusion bounded by experiential heuristics and observational learning.
*   **Sequential Representation**: 
    1. **Farmer 1 (Pioneer)** chooses: {Invest, Not Invest}.
    2. *If Invest*, system outcome is realized: {Success, Failure} (dependent on AS1 coordination).
    3. **Farmer 2 (Observer)** observes the outcome and chooses: {Imitate, Not Imitate}.
    4. *Decision Rule*: Farmer 2 imitates only if the observed outcome strictly ranks higher than their current baseline.
*   **Justification**: Grounded in AS2. Represents the sequential social-learning process where diffusion of capacitor adoption occurs only after a successful coordinated trial is observed by a peer, relying on bounded rationality rather than formal predictive models.

**Action Situation 3: Asymmetric Transformer-Capacity Authorization Dilemma**
*   **Title**: Asymmetric Transformer-Capacity Authorization Dilemma (Farmer-Farmer)
*   **Tension**: Asymmetric free-rider dilemma and uneven cost-sharing for collective infrastructure upgrades.
*   **Matrix**: 
    | Farmer 1 \ Farmer 2 | Authorize/Invest | Not Authorize |
    | :--- | :---: | :---: |
    | **Authorize/Invest** | 3, 3 | 1, 4 |
    | **Not Authorize** | 4, 1 | 2, 2 |
*   **Justification**: Grounded in AS3. One farmer’s authorization benefits both by raising voltage quality, but costs fall solely on the authorizer. If only one invests, the contributor bears the cost (1) while the non-investor benefits more (4). If neither invests, both remain at a low but non-zero baseline (2,2).

**Action Situation 4: Mutual-Exchange Coordination Game**
*   **Title**: Mutual-Exchange Coordination Game (Farmer-Staff)
*   **Tension**: Mutual exchange coordination requiring matched cooperation for reciprocal relational benefit.
*   **Matrix**: 
    | Farmer \ Sub-station Staff | Engage in Exchange | Abstain |
    | :--- | :---: | :---: |
    | **Engage in Exchange** | 3, 3 | 1, 2 |
    | **Abstain** | 2, 1 | 2, 2 |
*   **Justification**: Grounded in AS4. Reciprocal benefit arises only when both engage in informal exchange (3,3). If one abstains while the other offers, the offerer bears a loss (1) while the abstainer reverts to baseline (2). Mutual abstention yields no extra benefit (2,2).

**Action Situation 5: Authorization-and-Investment Asymmetric Coordination**
*   **Title**: Authorization-and-Investment Asymmetric Coordination (Farmer-Staff)
*   **Tension**: Asymmetric incentives between legality (formal requests) and opportunism (informal requests) under institutional and investment burdens.
*   **Matrix**: 
    | Farmer \ Sub-station Staff | Invest Capacity | Withhold Capacity |
    | :--- | :---: | :---: |
    | **Formal Request** | 4, 3 | 1, 4 |
    | **Informal Request** | 5, 1 | 2, 2 |
*   **Justification**: Grounded in AS5. Mutual formal cooperation is collectively optimal (4,3), though staff gain modestly due to the investment burden. If the farmer makes a formal request and staff withhold, the farmer incurs a loss (1) while staff save effort (4). If the farmer requests informally and staff invest, the farmer gains more (5) while staff bear the cost without a formal fee (1). Mutual informal withholding results in a baseline (2,2).

**Action Situation 6: Groundwater-Extraction Prisoner’s Dilemma**
*   **Title**: Groundwater-Extraction Prisoner’s Dilemma (Farmer-Farmer)
*   **Tension**: Common-pool resource extraction and the tragedy of the commons, where individual rationality leads to collective resource depletion.
*   **Matrix**: 
    | Farmer 1 \ Farmer 2 | Restrain Extraction | Over-extract |
    | :--- | :---: | :---: |
    | **Restrain Extraction** | 3, 3 | 1, 4 |
    | **Over-extract** | 4, 1 | 2, 2 |
*   **Justification**: Grounded in AS6. Mutual restraint sustains groundwater yields (3,3). Unilateral over-extraction offers short-term individual gain (4) but accelerates aquifer depletion, leaving the restrained farmer with a low yield (1). Mutual over-extraction depletes the resource, leaving both with a degraded baseline (2,2).
