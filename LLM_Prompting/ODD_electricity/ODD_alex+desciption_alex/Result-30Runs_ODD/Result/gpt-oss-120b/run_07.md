|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Farmer A Adopt** | (3 , 3) – mutual improvement | (1 , 2) – adopter bears cost, neighbour gets baseline |
| **Farmer A Not**   | (2 , 1) – neighbour bears cost, adopter gets baseline | (2 , 2) – status‑quo, low voltage |

*Justification:* Directly matches **AS1** in the ODD+D (“capacitor‑adoption assurance game between two neighbouring farmers”). The ordinal pay‑offs reflect the Pareto‑dominant cooperative outcome and the risky unilateral outcome.

**Action‑Situation 2 – Sequential Social‑Learning in Capacitor Adoption (Farmer → Farmer)**  
*Strategic tension:* Sequential diffusion – a farmer first decides to adopt; the second farmer observes the first’s realised outcome (high‑rank success or low‑rank failure) and then decides whether to imitate.  

**Game tree (compact):**  

1. **Farmer 1** chooses **Adopt** or **Not**.  
   - If **Adopt**, the outcome is observed as **Success** (rank = high) with probability *p* or **Failure** (rank = low) with probability *(1‑p)*.  
2. **Farmer 2** observes the realised outcome (Success/Failure) and then chooses **Adopt** or **Not** according to a simple rule:  
   - **If Success** → adopt (expects high payoff).  
   - **If Failure** → not adopt (avoids cost).  

*Justification:* This representation follows **AS2** (“sequential social‑learning process in capacitor adoption”) and captures the information‑asymmetric, time‑ordered decision making described in the ODD+D.

|----------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| **Farmer A Authorize** | (2 , 2) – shared benefit, cost split (both pay) | (1 , 3) – A bears full cost, B enjoys improved voltage |
| **Farmer A Not**       | (3 , 1) – B bears cost, A enjoys benefit | (2 , 2) – no upgrade, low‑quality service for both |

*Justification:* Mirrors **AS3** (“asymmetric transformer‑capacity authorization dilemma”) where the strategic asymmetry lies in who pays for the capacity increase versus who enjoys the voltage improvement.

**Action‑Situation 5 – Authorization‑and‑Investment Asymmetric Coordination (Farmer ↔ Staff)**  
*Strategic tension:* Formal‑vs‑informal request – the farmer can request a formal (fee‑based) or informal (no‑fee) connection; the staff can invest (provide capacity) or withhold. Payoffs are asymmetric because the staff bears investment costs while the farmer may reap larger gains from informal requests.  

|                | **Staff Invest** | **Staff Withhold** |
|----------------|------------------|--------------------|
| **Farmer Formal**   | (2 , 2) – fee paid, capacity provided (both incur moderate cost) | (1 , 3) – farmer pays fee but receives no capacity (farmer loss, staff saves effort) |
| **Farmer Informal** | (3 , 1) – farmer gets capacity free, staff bears cost (staff loss) | (2 , 2) – status‑quo, no fee, no extra capacity (both moderate) |

*Justification:* Implements **AS5**, the “authorization‑and‑investment asymmetric coordination game” between a farmer (formal vs. informal request) and utility staff (invest vs. withhold), reflecting the described asymmetry in incentives.
